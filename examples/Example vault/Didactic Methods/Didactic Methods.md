# Didactic Methods

Each section of a study note uses one or more of these methods and ends with a label that links here. **Active recall and spaced repetition matter most; the rest make them easier to do.**

| Method | Section of a note | What it is | How well supported |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[Pretesting]] | Before you read | Trying to answer questions about something before you learn it, even when you get them wrong. | Moderate to strong |
| [[Advance organizer]] | Start here (1 minute) | A short overview given before the details, so the new material has a frame to hang on. | Small to moderate |
| [[Progressive disclosure]] | Start here (1 minute) | Showing the essentials first and keeping the depth one step away, so you choose how far to go. | Design principle |
| [[Dual coding]] | Picture it | Pairing words with a simple picture of the same idea, such as a flowchart or a table. | Moderate |
| [[Chunking]] | The idea | Splitting material into a few small, named pieces. | Moderate |
| [[Elaboration]] | In my life | Connecting new material to what you already know and to your own situation: why is this true, where would I use it? | Moderate |
| [[Active recall]] | Check yourself | Pulling an answer out of memory instead of re-reading it. | Strong |
| [[Confidence calibration]] | Check yourself | Rating how well you actually know something, and checking that rating against your answers. | Moderate |
| [[Spaced repetition]] | Cards | Reviewing at growing intervals instead of all at once. | Strong |
| [[Teach-back]] | Teach it back | Explaining the idea simply, as if to someone new to it, without looking (also called the Feynman technique). | Moderate |
| [[Implementation intentions]] | Actions | Turning an intention into a when-where-what plan: "When X happens, I will do Y". | Strong (for follow-through) |
| [[Concept linking]] | Concepts used | Linking each note to the shared ideas it uses, so related material connects. | Moderate |
